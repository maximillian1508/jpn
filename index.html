The trip at a glance

Mid-January is Tokyo's secret season: the sunniest, driest month, the emptiest window of the winter, the best Mt Fuji visibility of the year. The trade is cold nights, early sunsets and very dry air — all planned around.

Who
A group of four, most of it on the same flight in.
Flights
Land at Narita Terminal 1 at 07:45 on Thursday the 14th; fly home from Terminal 1 on the morning of Saturday the 23rd. Flights & stay has both ends.
Base
A private floor of a house near Kanegafuchi Station on the Tobu Skytree Line, then one night in a manga cafe near Ueno.
Day trips
GALA Yuzawa skiing on Friday the 15th; Kamakura and Enoshima on Saturday the 16th; Mt Fuji and Kawaguchiko on Wednesday the 20th. Each has a Tokyo day either side — Plan A vs B shows the pass week that was weighed and why it wasn't picked.

Time
Japan is UTC+9, barely an hour off Southeast Asia — no jet lag to pay for the overnight flight.

Why mid-January

The calendar does a lot of the work.

The New Year rush is over by 5 January and Coming of Age Day falls on 11 January 2027, so domestic crowds have cleared before arrival. Chinese New Year is 6 February 2027, so the regional wave hasn't started either: shorter queues, softer rates, no festival surcharges.

It's also Tokyo's driest, clearest month — the best Fuji visibility of the year, which pays off twice: from the free Metropolitan Government deck on Day 6, and for the whole Kawaguchiko trip on Day 7.

The trade-off is daylight and cold. The sun sets around 16:50, so outdoor and view-dependent sights go first and evenings are for food, shopping and bars; nights drop to near freezing, which Packing handles.

January weather

What it actually feels like.

Daytime8–11°C. Sunny and still — pleasant with one good layer. Kawaguchiko runs 5–8 degrees colder.
Night0–3°C, occasionally below. This is where it bites, especially standing still.
RainTokyo's lowest-rainfall month. Snow in the centre is possible but uncommon.
Sunset~16:50 mid-month. Front-load outdoor sights; evenings are for food, shopping and bars.
HumidityAround 30–40%, against the tropics' 80%. Dry air, not cold, is what gets you: cracked lips, dry nose, static, itchy skin. Buy lip balm and a heavy moisturiser on day one — any Matsumoto Kiyoshi has both.

Why the listing's licence number matters

Private lodging in Japan runs under the minpaku law: a licensed home can be rented out at most 180 nights a year and has to show its licence number on the listing. Unlicensed listings do get taken down, sometimes weeks before arrival, and the cancellation lands on the guest. Checking the number now is how to avoid finding out in January; Packing has the fallback.

Before anyone flies

A handful of things need booking, one thing can stop you boarding.

JAVES is not optional

Indonesian passports are not visa-free by default. The 15-day visa waiver only works with an e-passport (chip symbol on the cover) registered in advance through JAVES, Japan's online exemption system. It's free, opens 14 weeks before arrival (8 October 2026 for a 14 January arrival), is normally processed within 2 working days of the embassy receiving it, and lasts 3 years or until the passport expires. Airlines check it at the check-in counter; no registration means no boarding.

Register on JAVES · JAVES FAQ

One quirk: the approval notice must be shown live from your phone (email or the JAVES account) — printouts and screenshots are not accepted. That's the opposite of Visit Japan Web, where screenshots of your QR codes are exactly what you want.

How JAVES works

  • Register through the official JAVES system (linked from the MOFA Japan site). Create the account, fill in the details, upload passport scans — cover, biodata page, and endorsement page if any. Names and spacing must match the passport exactly.
  • Approval arrives by email as an electronic Visa Exemption Registration Notice, normally within 2 working days of the embassy receiving the application.
  • One account, or a proxy, can apply for the others, but every passport needs its own registration — and everyone first checks the passport has the chip symbol on the cover — a regular passport can't use JAVES and needs a full visa application instead.
  • Do it early. If the online route misbehaves there is still time for the paper route: a paper registration with a sticker in the passport, done in Jakarta through the Japan Visa Application Centre — or ask the nearest Japanese embassy.

What else to have ready

  • A passport valid for the whole trip (six months beyond return is the safe margin) — with the JAVES registration on that same passport. Renew it and the registration dies with the old one.
  • Proof of onward travel and your first-night address. The return ticket and the stay booking cover both; immigration officers on the 15-day waiver do sometimes ask, so keep them reachable.
  • Visit Japan Web (vjw.digital.go.jp), a week or two before flying. It turns immigration and customs into QR scans — screenshot both codes. Set up Visit Japan Web

What B would gain, and give up

  • Gain: ¥4,400 each; every outing still on a weekday; a reserved 07:30 train to Fuji instead of a 06:30 bus that sells out and a 05:20 alarm; a free Fuji weather swap with Tuesday's Kamakura; Kamakura's JR for free.
  • Give up: three out-of-town days in a row (ski Mon, Kamakura Tue, Fuji Wed — Kamakura the soft middle); Shinjuku on a Saturday night; DisneySea on day two; one more booking system and a pass pickup on arrival.

Why A: no three-outing block, Shinjuku on a Tuesday rather than a Saturday night, no pass logistics — for ¥4,400 more each. Both are good weeks.

How the days are built

Every heavy day is a weekday with a Tokyo day between: GALA on the Friday for fresh legs, DisneySea on the Monday, Fuji on the Wednesday — ski, city, city, park, city, mountain, city. Kamakura takes the Saturday because it needs no reservations and is the gentlest day trip, the right one for the morning after skiing; Yanaka, Ueno and Akihabara take the Sunday for Akiba's car-free afternoon. Times are guidance — the sequence matters more than the clock.

Sunset is around 16:50, so anything outdoors or view-dependent happens before then. There is no formal rest day, but no two heavy days touch. The relief valves: Saturday and Sunday stay near home with late starts, Tuesday ends at midnight by rule because of the 05:20 Fuji alarm, and Thursday's shrine stop is the thing to drop. Use them.

Eating well at every price

Japan is the rare country where cheap food is genuinely good. Roughly what each price band buys.

Under ¥1,000

Konbini7-Eleven, FamilyMart, Lawson. Onigiri ¥130–200, bento ¥450–750, egg sando ¥250. Famichiki fried chicken and melon pan are the cult items. Not a compromise.
GyudonYoshinoya, Matsuya, Sukiya. Beef bowl ¥450–650, 24 hours, ticket machine at the door.
Ramen¥800–1,300 almost anywhere. Ichiran's booths are the experience; the ramen itself is better elsewhere.
UdonMarugame Seimen from ¥400. Fast, hot, absurdly cheap.
CurryCoCo Ichibanya from ¥750, choose your heat and toppings.
SupermarketsPrepared food 20–50% off once the yellow stickers go on, around 19:00–20:00. The best-value food in Japan.

¥1,000–3,000

  • Lunch sets (teishoku) — the single best trick. Restaurants charging ¥6,000+ at dinner serve ¥1,500–2,500 sets at midday from the same kitchen. Eat the good meals at lunch.
  • Conveyor-belt sushi — ¥1,500–2,500 and you're full. Uobei and Genki Sushi are the reliable chains.
  • Izakaya — shared plates and drinks, ¥2,000–4,000 each; the default group dinner. Look for nomihodai (all-you-can-drink), ¥1,500–2,500 for two hours.
  • Tonkatsu, tempura, gyukatsu — ¥1,200–2,000 for a proper set.

¥3,000 and up

  • Yakiniku — Japanese BBQ, built for groups. All-you-can-eat ¥3,000–5,000. The best farewell-dinner format there is.
  • Sushi counter — lunch omakase from ¥3,000, dinner from ¥10,000. Do it at lunch.
  • A5 wagyu — from ¥5,000 for a steak. Worth doing once.
  • Day-trip dishes — hoto at Kawaguchiko and shirasu-don in Kamakura, both under ¥1,500 and half the point of going.

Good to know

  • Eat-in is taxed at 10%, takeaway at 8% — they will ask.
  • Winter-only things worth hunting: oden from the konbini counter, nabe hotpot, taiyaki from street stalls, hot canned coffee from any vending machine.

Shopping and the new tax refund

Where to go, how the refund works now, and how to get it all home.

The rules changed on 1 November 2026

Japan scrapped instant tax-free discounts at the register. You now pay the full price in store and claim the 10% back at a customs kiosk at the airport before flying. Any guide that says the tax comes off at the till is out of date.

Official guide for travellers

How the refund works

  • Spend ¥5,000+ before tax in one store on one day, and show your passport at the till so the purchase is recorded against it.
  • You pay the full tax-inclusive price. Keep every receipt.
  • At Narita before departure, scan your passport at the self-service customs kiosk, which pulls up your recorded purchases. Do it before checking in, with the goods in hand — customs may ask to see them. At Narita, Visit Japan Web can also handle this step online.
  • The refund is then paid by the store or its refund provider, usually back to the card you used. Timing varies by provider.
  • Board without doing the kiosk step and the refund is forfeited permanently.
  • Practical upshot: carry about 10% extra float while in Japan. The upside: no more sealed bags or separate consumables category, so skincare bought mid-trip can be used mid-trip — though anything consumed in Japan stops being eligible.

  • Pack light on the way out — the mid-trip coin-laundry wash frees most of a suitcase for the way home.
  • Know your checked-bag allowance and weigh the bag before the final shopping run. Excess is expensive at the airport and cheaper bought online beforehand.

  • Tax-free purchases and their receipts travel in the cabin bag on departure day; customs can't inspect what's already checked in.

Getting around

Home station is Kanegafuchi on the Tobu Skytree Line. Asakusa and Oshiage are the two hubs you'll transfer through constantly — learn that pattern on day one and the city opens up.

Kanegafuchi is deep east-side: about 20 minutes to Asakusa, 15 to Oshiage under the Skytree, 30 to Ueno, 40 to Tokyo Station, and roughly 50 to Shibuya or Shinjuku. The plan works with that, not against it — east-side days cluster near home, west-side nights are planned outings, and both day trips leave from major terminals anyway.

The last train shapes every evening

Trains stop around 00:30 and restart around 05:00; there is no night service. From Shinjuku or Shibuya, leaving by midnight is the difference between a train home and karaoke until dawn. A taxi split four ways is fine for short hops after midnight (GO is the app); a cross-town one is not — avoid needing it.

Suica and cash

  • A Suica or Pasmo covers trains, buses, konbini, vending machines and most chain restaurants with a tap. Load ¥5,000 on arrival. iPhones can run Mobile Suica in Apple Wallet; most non-Japanese Androids can't, so take the physical Welcome Suica at the airport — valid 28 days, no deposit. About Welcome Suica
  • Carry ¥10,000–15,000 cash at all times. Golden Gai, small restaurants, temples and market stalls are cash-only.

  • Check your card's foreign transaction fee before relying on it — some charge 1–3% plus a markup.

Etiquette that actually matters

  • No tipping, ever — it confuses staff. Many small places use a ticket machine at the door: pay, take the ticket, hand it over, sit.
  • Don't eat while walking; stand still or sit.
  • Quiet on trains, no calls. Stand left on escalators. Carry your rubbish — public bins barely exist, so keep a plastic bag in the daypack.
  • Shoes off where indicated, and always inside the house.
  • Visible tattoos can bar entry to onsen and sento; cover patches help, but check first.

Packing for a January Tokyo

A ten-degree swing between afternoon and night, one mountain day colder still. Layers solve it; one heavy coat does not.

Base2–3 thermal base layers. Buy Uniqlo Heattech Extra Warm in Tokyo rather than before the trip — cheaper there.
Mid3–4 long-sleeve shirts, sweaters or fleeces — the layer you add and remove all day.
OuterOne insulated or down jacket. Wind-resistant matters more than heavy; the Kawaguchiko ropeway deck is the windiest place on this trip.
Legs2 pairs of jeans or trousers, thermal leggings underneath for the Fuji day, GALA and the DisneySea evening. Waterproof over-pants for GALA only if not renting the wear set — Don Quijote Asakusa on day one, ~¥2,000–3,000. No jeans under ski wear.
ExtremitiesBeanie, two pairs of gloves (one waterproof, for the snow), scarf, 5+ pairs of warm socks. Cheap, and they do more than a heavier coat.
ShoesWaterproof and comfortable, easy to slip off: 15,000–20,000 steps a day, and they come off at every temple and at the house.
One good outfitFor the farewell dinner and any club night — clubs turn away sportswear.

Tech and documents

  • Passport, live JAVES access on the phone, screenshots of both Visit Japan Web QR codes.
  • Type A plug adapter — Japan uses the flat two-pin, so three-pin plugs won't fit. Japan runs 100V; modern chargers handle it.
  • Power bank. Cold drains phone batteries noticeably faster, and you'll navigate all day.
  • eSIM activation details and copies of bookings, saved offline.

For anyone coming from the tropics

  • Heavy moisturiser, lip balm and hand cream from day one. Knuckles crack otherwise.
  • Basic medicine: paracetamol, antacid, plasters. Japanese pharmacies stock equivalents, but the packaging is all in Japanese.

Deliberately not packing

  • Ten days of clothes. Pack five or six and wash mid-trip.
  • An umbrella — driest month, and any konbini sells one for ¥500.
  • Full-size toiletries. Drugstores are cheaper and better, and it's shopping you were going to do anyway.

If things go wrong

Fuji is hidingWednesday 20 is tentative by design. Decide on Tuesday evening from the Kawaguchiko live cams and forecast: if it's hopeless, rebook the bus to Thursday (seats permitting) and swap with Harajuku / Shibuya — Shibuya Sky rebooks and dinner is one call. No Thursday seats: go Wednesday anyway, Chureito first. January clarity means this mostly won't happen.
Missed the first train to GALAThe 09:00 lesson is lost — Giant doesn't extend or refund for late arrival. The 12:00 slot [2B] only if an instructor happens to be free. The 05:30 alarm is not negotiable.
Someone is wrecked after GALAThe morning after is Kamakura, by design: no bookings, a 07:45 start and lots of sitting on the Enoden. Start late, skip Enoshima, keep the Buddha and the cape. And nobody buys a lift ticket until their travel insurance is confirmed to cover skiing.
RainUnlikely. Sunday (museum, Ameyoko, arcades) and Tuesday (Isetan) absorb a wet day, and DisneySea runs in rain. Shuffle a soaked outdoor day toward museums, arcades and department stores rather than cancelling it.
Snow in central TokyoPossible, uncommon. Trains slow but rarely stop. It's a photo opportunity at Senso-ji more than a problem — waterproof shoes handle it.
DisneySea sells out for the 18thMove it to Tuesday 19 and Shinjuku to Monday 18 — nothing on either day is booked except dinner. Never the weekend.
Someone gets sickDrugstores cover the basics; bigger hospitals have English-speaking staff. Japanese healthcare is excellent and expensive — this is what travel insurance is for.
Money runs shortCut the shopping, never the experiences. Uniqlo exists at home; the Fuji day doesn't. Food can drop to ¥3,000 a day without suffering.
Group money frictionSplit fixed costs (the stay, group dinners), keep everything discretionary individual. A shared pot for nine days never survives four people.
Missed last trainKaraoke until 05:00 is the cheap answer, a taxi split four ways the fast one, a capsule hotel or manga cafe (¥3,000–5,000) the comfortable one. All normal.
The house listing is taken downThe booking platform rebooks or refunds. Fall back on a hostel or business hotel near Ueno or Kuramae — the per-night rates are in Flights & stay.
Running late at NaritaThe flight beats the refund. Abandon the kiosk queue if it comes to that — missing the plane costs far more than 10%.
