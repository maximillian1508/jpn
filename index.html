Group trip plan for four

TOKYO東京 · 冬

14 – 23 January 2027. Nine nights: eight in a house in Sumida, one in a manga cafe.

Mid-January is Tokyo's secret season. The sunniest, driest month of the year, the emptiest window between New Year and Chinese New Year, and the best Mt Fuji visibility you can get. The trade: cold nights, 4:50pm sunsets, and air so dry it cracks your lips. All of that is planned around below.

Private floor of a house, 7 minutes' walk from Kanegafuchi Station (Tobu Skytree Line), checked in 14–22 Jan.
Last night
Checkout is the morning of the 22nd. Final night is a manga cafe near Ueno, because the first airport train leaves from there at 05:58.
Departure
Morning of Saturday the 23rd from Narita Terminal 1.
Weather
Days 8–11°C and sunny. Nights 0–3°C. Rain unlikely. Kawaguchiko runs 5–8 degrees colder than the city.

Before anyone flies

Four things need booking, one thing can stop you boarding.

JAVES is not optional

Indonesian passports are not visa-free by default. The 15-day visa waiver only works with an e-passport (chip symbol on the cover) registered in advance through JAVES, Japan's online exemption system. It's free, takes 2–4 working days, and lasts 3 years or until the passport expires. Airlines check it at the gate; no registration means no boarding.

One quirk: the approval notice must be shown live from your phone (email or the JAVES account) — printouts and screenshots are not accepted. That's the opposite of Visit Japan Web, where screenshots of your QR codes are exactly what you want. Do both before flying: JAVES now, Visit Japan Web (vjw.digital.go.jp) a week or two out.

  • JAVES registration, every person individually, done well ahead
  • Tokyo Disneyland tickets for Tuesday 19 January, bought the moment the date opens
  • teamLab Planets, midday slot, Wednesday 20 January — sells out weeks ahead
  • Kawaguchiko highway bus, both directions, Monday 18 January (highway-buses.jp, opens ~1 month out, genuinely sells out)
  • Timed-entry extras once dates are certain: Tokyo Skytree (evening of the 14th) and Shibuya Sky (sunset on the 21st)
  • Dinner reservations: shabu-shabu in Shibuya for the 21st, farewell yakiniku for the 22nd

The house, and its rules

A private floor of a family home in a quiet residential street — the real-Tokyo experience, with real-Tokyo etiquette.

Check-inFrom 16:00, self check-in by smart-lock PIN (sent before arrival). Nobody is on site.
CheckoutBy 10:00 on the 22nd. No desk, so no bag storage — plan on station coin lockers either side of the stay.
BedsTwo double beds for four people. Agree the pairs before night one, not during it.
LaundryNo washer in the house. Coin laundry nearby: ¥400–600 a load, about an hour. Slotted for Sunday night or the slow Wednesday morning — pack five or six days of clothes, not ten.
House rulesNon-smoking. Shoes off inside. Quiet after 23:00 — the neighbours are real people, so late returns happen at a whisper.
NearbyFamilyMart and 7-Eleven in walking distance, a good seafood izakaya (Uoma) by the station, and Mimatsuyu, a proper local sento (public bath) — the cheap, authentic answer to sore legs.

Getting around from here

Kanegafuchi is deep east-side: about 20 minutes to Asakusa, 15 to Oshiage under the Skytree, 30 to Ueno, 40 to Tokyo Station, and roughly 50 to Shibuya or Shinjuku. The plan works with that, not against it — east-side days cluster near home, west-side nights are planned outings. The number that matters: trains stop around 00:30 and restart at 05:00, so leaving Shinjuku or Shibuya by midnight is the difference between a train home and karaoke until dawn. Decide which before the night starts.

Money, transport, apps

  • Get a Suica on arrival and load ¥5,000 — it covers trains, buses, konbini and vending machines. iPhones can run Mobile Suica in Apple Wallet; most non-Japanese Androids can't, so take the physical Welcome Suica.
  • Carry ¥10,000–15,000 cash at all times. Golden Gai, small restaurants, temples and market stalls are cash-only. 7-Bank ATMs (inside 7-Eleven) take foreign cards reliably; fewer, larger withdrawals beat many small ones.
  • Skip the JR Pass — nothing on this plan needs it. Kamakura is a ¥950 local ride and Fuji goes by bus.
  • Trains stop ~00:30, restart ~05:00. A taxi split four ways is fine for short hops after midnight; a cross-town one is not. GO is the taxi app.
  • Install before flying: Google Maps (offline Tokyo), Google Translate (offline Japanese, camera mode), Visit Japan Web, Tabelog (anything above 3.5 is genuinely good — the scale is harsh), GO, the Tokyo Disney Resort app.

Etiquette that actually matters

  • No tipping, ever. Many small places use a ticket machine at the door: pay, take the ticket, hand it over, sit.
  • Quiet on trains, no calls. Stand left on escalators. Carry your rubbish — public bins barely exist.
  • Shoes off where indicated, and always inside the house.
  • Visible tattoos can bar entry to onsen and sento; cover patches help, but check first.

Eating well on any budget

Japan is the rare country where cheap food is genuinely good. Konbini breakfasts (onigiri ¥130–200, egg sando ¥250) are what everyone does, not a compromise. Gyudon chains run ¥450–650 around the clock; ramen is ¥800–1,300 almost anywhere; supermarkets discount prepared food 20–50% after 19:00.

The single best trick: eat the good meals at lunch. Restaurants charging ¥6,000+ at dinner serve ¥1,500–2,500 lunch sets from the same kitchen. Group dinners default to izakaya (¥2,000–4,000 each, look for ¥1,500–2,500 nomihodai) or all-you-can-eat yakiniku. Winter-only things worth hunting: konbini oden, nabe hotpot, taiyaki from street stalls, hot canned coffee from any vending machine — and the day-trip dishes, hoto noodles at Kawaguchiko and shirasu-don in Kamakura.

Eat-in is taxed at 10%, takeaway at 8% — they will ask. Halal options are limited but real (Halal Navi lists them, mostly around Shinjuku, Asakusa and Ueno); vegetarian is harder than it looks because dashi hides in everything.

Shopping and the new tax refund

The rules changed on 1 November 2026

Japan scrapped instant tax-free discounts at the register. You now pay the full price in store and claim the 10% back at a customs kiosk at the airport before flying. Any guide that says the tax comes off at the till is out of date. Spend ¥5,000+ in one store in one day, show your passport so it's recorded, keep every receipt, keep the goods reachable in cabin bags on departure day — and know that boarding without doing the kiosk step forfeits the refund permanently. Practical upshot: budget ~10% extra float while in Japan, and no more sealed bags, so skincare bought mid-trip can be used mid-trip.

Packing for a January Tokyo

Cold but dry, with a ten-degree swing between afternoon and night, and one mountain day colder still. Layers solve it; one heavy coat does not. Pack five or six days of clothes — the coin laundry handles the rest and the empty space comes home full of shopping.

  • 2–3 thermal base layers (buy Heattech Extra Warm in Tokyo), 3–4 mid layers, one wind-resistant insulated jacket.
  • Thermal leggings for the Fuji day and the Disneyland parade wait. Beanie, gloves, scarf, 5+ pairs of warm socks — cheap items that outperform a heavier coat.
  • Waterproof, comfortable shoes that slip off easily: 15,000–20,000 steps a day, and they come off at every temple and at the house.
  • Type A plug adapter (Japan's flat two-pin; 100V, fine for modern chargers), power bank (cold drains batteries), passport plus live JAVES access plus Visit Japan Web screenshots.
  • Heavy moisturiser, lip balm, hand cream from day one — the dry air, not the cold, is what nobody from the tropics is ready for.
  • Skip the umbrella (driest month; konbini sell them for ¥500) and full-size toiletries (drugstores are better and cheaper).

If things go wrong

Fuji is hidingCheck the Kawaguchiko live cams the night before and at 06:00. Solid cloud: swap the Fuji day with Thursday — Shibuya Sky slots rebook easily, the dinner reservation is one call, and the bus can usually be changed once. January clarity means this mostly won't happen.
RainUnlikely, and Wednesday is already the indoor day. Shuffle a soaked outdoor day toward museums, arcades and department stores rather than cancelling it.
Disneyland sells out for the 19thMove it to Wednesday and shift teamLab to Tuesday — teamLab slots move far more easily than Disney dates. Never the weekend.
Someone gets sickDrugstores cover the basics; bigger hospitals have English-speaking staff. Japanese healthcare is excellent and expensive — this is what everyone's travel insurance is for.
Money runs shortCut the shopping, never the experiences. Uniqlo exists at home; the Fuji day doesn't. Food can drop to ¥3,000 a day without suffering.
Group money frictionSplit fixed costs (the stay, group dinners), keep everything discretionary individual. A shared pot for nine days never survives four people.
Missed last trainKaraoke until 05:00 is the cheap answer, a taxi split four ways the fast one, a capsule hotel or manga cafe (¥3,000–5,000) the comfortable one. All normal.
Running late at NaritaThe flight beats the refund. Abandon the kiosk queue if it comes to that — missing the plane costs far more than 10%.
